Case study

Mouse gut metaproteome: 20% yeast vs 20% casein protein

This page runs the repdist workflow on a published metaproteome: beta diversity with MMD (sample_mmd(), the maximum mean discrepancy under a Gaussian RBF kernel on the protein embeddings), the representation space, MCL bins, and bin-level differential abundance.

library(repdist)
library(ggplot2)
library(vegan)

Study and comparison

Blakeley-Ruiz et al. (Blakeley-Ruiz et al. 2025) fed twelve mice (two cages of three per sex) a series of defined diets, one week each, and measured faecal metaproteomes by spectral counting (PRIDE PXD041586).

The input sequences are already sequence-reduced. The microbial protein catalog comes from metagenome-assembled genomes annotated with PROKKA; unbinned genes were added only if no binned gene matched them at 90% identity (CD-HIT-2D), and the host and diet reference proteomes were clustered at 95% identity. Every reported protein also has at least one protein-unique peptide. Near-identical proteins are therefore rare in the table (see sequence identity of graph edges): the relatedness that MMD and the bins add beyond protein identity is mostly remote homology. This is consistent with the modest difference between MMD and protein-level Bray-Curtis below.

This page compares two diets of the same protein amount: 20% yeast protein (Yeast) against 20% casein (Casein), casein being the reference protein of the series. Only mice sampled on both diets are used (11 pairs), and diet is tested within mouse. The yeast diet came three weeks after the casein diet, so diet is confounded with feeding period in this serial design.

d <- readRDS(file.path("curated", "diet_yeast.rds"))

The curated object was built by data-raw/diet_yeast.R from the paper’s supplementary Data Set 1 (PSM counts and sequences) and Data Set 6 (annotation): every microbial protein with at least one PSM in these 22 samples, its species group and GTDB lineage, the study’s curated function, KEGG orthology (KO) assignments and CAZy families, and a TM-Vec embedding computed with embed_proteins().

Protein length. Proteins longer than MAX_AA = 1500 residues were removed before embedding: 162 of 11205 proteins, carrying 1.9% of the PSMs. This is an analysis choice, not a package limit: embed_proteins() embeds sequences of any length, but the TM-Vec head was trained on chains of up to 1000 residues, so predicted TM-scores for much longer chains are extrapolations.

Beta diversity

Sequencing depth differs by diet (yeast samples have about twice the PSMs of casein samples), and depth changes which proteins are observed. Both distances are therefore computed on counts rarefied to the smallest sample.

tapply(rowSums(counts), samples$diet, range)
#> $Casein
#> [1]  6580 18758
#> 
#> $Yeast
#> [1] 19715 30594
set.seed(1)
rarefied <- rrarefy(counts, min(rowSums(counts)))
D <- list(
    `Bray-Curtis (protein)` = vegdist(rarefied, "bray"),
    MMD = sample_mmd(rarefied, x)
)
attr(D$MMD, "sigma")
#> [1] 1.145504

PERMANOVA (Anderson 2001) and PERMDISP (Anderson 2006) use 999 permutations restricted to within mouse.

perm <- permute::how(blocks = samples$subject, nperm = 999)
tests <- t(vapply(D, function(Dk) {
    set.seed(1)
    a <- adonis2(Dk ~ diet, data = samples, permutations = perm)
    bd <- betadisper(Dk, samples$diet)
    set.seed(1)
    pd <- permutest(bd, permutations = perm)
    c(R2 = a$R2[1], F = a$F[1], p = a$`Pr(>F)`[1],
        dispersion_Casein = mean(bd$distances[samples$diet == "Casein"]),
        dispersion_Yeast = mean(bd$distances[samples$diet == "Yeast"]),
        PERMDISP_p = pd$tab[1, "Pr(>F)"])
}, numeric(6)))

Each grey line joins the casein and yeast samples of one mouse.

Both distances separate the diets with similar effect sizes, and for both PERMDISP also detects unequal spread, in opposite directions: casein samples are more dispersed under Bray-Curtis, yeast samples under MMD. PERMANOVA with equal group sizes is fairly robust to unequal dispersion, and the paired samples are displaced along the first axis in both ordinations, so the separation is mainly a shift in location, with a difference in spread on top. Under Bray-Curtis the second axis separates the two mouse groups, which came from different vendor rooms.

Representation space

plot_tsne() maps all proteins by cosine distance (one minus the predicted TM-score), coloured by the study’s manually curated broad function; proteins without one are grey. The map shows neighbourhoods in representation space; it is not evidence of function, and t-SNE does not preserve distances between groups.

set.seed(1)
plot_tsne(x, color = setNames(ann$broad_function, ann$protein))

Differential abundance

Raw (unrarefied) protein counts are summed to bins with bin_glom() and tested with DESeq2 (Love et al. 2014). The design ~ subject + diet pairs the two samples of each mouse; size factors use the poscounts estimator because no bin is observed in every sample. Fold changes are Yeast relative to Casein, shrunk with the normal prior for display; p-values are from the Wald test.

binned <- bin_glom(counts, b)
dds <- DESeq2::DESeqDataSetFromMatrix(round(t(binned)), samples,
    design = ~ subject + diet)
dds <- DESeq2::DESeq(dds, sfType = "poscounts", quiet = TRUE)
res <- DESeq2::results(dds, name = "diet_Yeast_vs_Casein", alpha = 0.05)
lfc <- DESeq2::lfcShrink(dds, coef = "diet_Yeast_vs_Casein", type = "normal",
    res = res, quiet = TRUE)

Each bin’s annotation summarises its members. A label shared by every annotated member is shown with its count (3/4 means three of four members carry it, the fourth is unannotated); bins whose annotated members disagree are marked mixed with the number of distinct labels and the most frequent one.

summarise_labels <- function(v) {
    n <- length(v)
    v <- v[!is.na(v)]
    if (!length(v)) return("-")
    tab <- sort(table(v), decreasing = TRUE)
    top <- sprintf("%s (%d/%d)", strtrim(names(tab)[1], 40), tab[[1]], n)
    if (length(tab) == 1L) top else
        sprintf("mixed, %d: %s", length(tab), top)
}
bin_table <- function(bins) {
    data.frame(
        bin = bins,
        proteins = sizes[bins],
        taxon = vapply(b$clusters[bins],
            function(m) summarise_labels(ann[m, "taxon"]), ""),
        `function` = vapply(b$clusters[bins],
            function(m) summarise_labels(ann[m, "consensus_annotation"]), ""),
        KO = vapply(b$clusters[bins],
            function(m) summarise_labels(ann[m, "ko"]), ""),
        log2FC = round(lfc[bins, "log2FoldChange"], 2),
        p = formatC(res[bins, "pvalue"], format = "e", digits = 1),
        padj = formatC(res[bins, "padj"], format = "e", digits = 1),
        check.names = FALSE, row.names = NULL
    )
}

Inside a bin

plot_bin_similarity() shows all pairwise predicted TM-scores within one bin. A black outline marks a pair that is an edge of the graph: it passed both thresholds (predicted TM-score ≥ 0.7, coverage ≥ 0.5) and keeps its similarity as the edge weight. A cell without an outline is not an edge (weight 0 in S). MCL clusters along the outlined edges. Shown are the most significant bin with 4 to 30 members in each direction, with a count of their member pairs: edges, pairs below the similarity threshold, and pairs above it that failed the coverage gate.

pick <- function(bins) bins[sizes[bins] >= 4 & sizes[bins] <= 30][1]
shown <- c(Yeast = pick(sig[lfc[sig, "log2FoldChange"] > 0]),
    Casein = pick(sig[lfc[sig, "log2FoldChange"] < 0]))
pair_types <- function(bin) {
    m <- b$clusters[[bin]]
    R <- tcrossprod(Z[m, ])
    E <- S[m, m] > 0
    up <- upper.tri(E)
    c(pairs = sum(up), edges = sum(E[up]), below_0.7 = sum(R[up] < 0.7),
        failed_coverage = sum(R[up] >= 0.7 & !E[up]))
}

In the GH92 bin every pair of members is an edge. In bin209, mostly unannotated proteins from several Acetatifactor species groups and unbinned contigs, some members are joined only through shared neighbours (pairs below 0.7) and some similar pairs are not edges because their alignment covers less than half of one sequence. MCL keeps both kinds of pair in one bin; the thresholds act on edges, not on every pair.

Notes

  • Diet is confounded with feeding period (weeks 2 and 5) in this serial design, so the differences cannot be attributed to diet alone.
  • Beta diversity uses rarefied counts; differential abundance uses raw counts with DESeq2 size factors.
  • A bin is a similarity-network cluster. Agreement among its members’ annotations is evidence about its function; bins can span species and, when large, related functions.
  • KO assignments are the study’s own (Mantis, else MicrobeAnnotator). A missing KO does not mean an unknown function, as the GH92 bin shows.
