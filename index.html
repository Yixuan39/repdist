repdist / analyses

Re-analyses of published metaproteomes with homology-aware beta diversity: each sample as an abundance-weighted distribution over protein language model embeddings, compared by maximum mean discrepancy.

Reports

  1. 01Data curation

    Extended Data Table 1 of Blakeley-Ruiz et al., ISME J 19(1) wraf048, from spectral counts and inline sequences to a phyloseq object, checked against the published PERMANOVA before anything downstream runs.

  2. 02Case study — mouse faecal metaproteomes

    Six dietary protein sources. MMD beta diversity and PERMANOVA, structural bins at predicted TM-score 0.7, DESeq2 on the bins, and a functional reading of the most responsive annotated bins — on every protein retained after rarefaction and a 3,000 aa length ceiling.

  3. 03Case study 2 — marine plastic substrates

    Three incubations each across four plastics, two PHBH formulations, filter paper and ambient sediment; PLA dropped for inconsistent depth. The same four-step workflow on a community whose proteins are largely unannotated.

  4. 04Case study 2 — annotation-oriented clustering

    Which clustering setting groups the plastic-substrate catalog best for annotation: complete linkage across TM thresholds against MCL, DBSCAN*/DBSCAN and HDBSCAN, scored on KO and on the paper's own broad and detailed function labels. historical

Reproducing

The .Rmd and .R sources sit in inst/scripts/ on this branch and expect to run from a repdist checkout, with the study inputs under data/. Those inputs are not redistributed here; each report names its source files and the accession they come from. Report 04 also needs results/case_study2_annotation_clustering/, written by inst/scripts/case_study2_annotation.R.

Report 04 is kept as a record: the MCL, DBSCAN*/DBSCAN and HDBSCAN options it compares have since been removed from bin_proteins(), which now cuts a complete-linkage tree and nothing else. Its own conclusion is the reason.

Package source, installation and the two vignettes live on github.com/Yixuan39/repdist. This site is built from the gh-pages branch.
